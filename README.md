# Korean Talchum Mask Dance Traditions Registry

This repository preserves, in an immutable and decentralized format, the mask dance traditions of **Talchum** — Korea’s ritual, theatrical, and satirical masked performances, recognized by UNESCO as Intangible Cultural Heritage.

Talchum combines carved wooden masks, stylized dance, pungmul percussion, satire, shamanic elements, and community participation. Each region has its own mask types, characters, rhythms, and narrative structures.

## Why Talchum Matters

Talchum represents:

- a **ritual performance tradition**, rooted in exorcism and harvest rites  
- a **theatrical art**, using satire to critique social hierarchies  
- a **regional mask‑making heritage**, with Hahoe, Bongsan, Yangju, and Gangneung styles  
- a **musical tradition**, driven by pungmul and samulnori rhythms  
- a **UNESCO‑recognized cultural practice**, inscribed in 2022  

Its masks, characters, and movements form one of Korea’s most iconic cultural expressions.

## Purpose of This Contract

The goal of this registry is to:

1. **Create a permanent, censorship-resistant record** of Talchum traditions.  
2. **Preserve endangered mask‑making and performance knowledge**.  
3. **Document masks, materials, dance styles, music, and cultural context**.  
4. **Provide an immutable reference** for researchers, performers, and future generations.  
5. **Honor the regional diversity of Korean mask dance culture.**

The contract is intentionally:

- **ownerless** — no authority can alter or censor the registry  
- **payment-free** — no monetization, no barriers  
- **minimalistic and secure** — no external calls  
- **permanent** — entries become part of the cultural ledger  

## What Each Entry Records

- **maskName** — Hahoe Yangban, Bongsan Monk, Bune, Gwanno, etc.  
- **region** — Hahoe, Bongsan, Yangju, Gangneung  
- **materials** — alder wood, lacquer, pigments  
- **danceStyle** — slow steps, circular motions, jumps  
- **musicalEnsemble** — pungmul, samulnori, drums, flutes  
- **culturalContext** — satire, rituals, festivals  
- **uniqueness** — regional identity, UNESCO status
